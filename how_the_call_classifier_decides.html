📡 What it is given

1
The transcript only
Labelled SPEAKER 0 and SPEAKER 1, nothing else.
2
It works out who is who
The salesperson is the one talking about products, prices, lead times and availability.
3
No outside help
No web access, no other calls, no CRM data.

📏 The rules it must follow

1
Same written instructions on every call
Never varied, so every call is judged the same way.
2
Answers only
No explanation, no opinion.
3
Seven answers, every one required
It cannot leave a blank.

🎯 How it lands on one

💬
What the prospect actually said, not how they sounded
Clear commitment is Confirmed. Interest without commitment is Thinking. A stated no is No.
❓
Doubt goes to Unclear
Anything it cannot place goes to Unclear rather than a guess, and a human picks it up.

📝 The other six things it writes down

🧾 SUMMARYThree or four sentences on where the call stands
🤝 CLOSER PROMISESWhat the salesperson committed to, or None
👤 CUSTOMER ACTIONSWhat the prospect said they would do, or None
📅 NEXT TOUCHDate and time agreed, or Not agreed
🧱 OBJECTIONSAnything raised, or None
🛁 PRODUCT DISCUSSEDProduct and spec, including any price given

📬 Where the answer goes next

1
The answer is posted into GHL
The Zap sends the seven answers to the system the moment they are written, and the outcome is stamped on the contact as a tag.
2
Two routers read it, and only one acts
The first-call router handles leads who have not yet booked with the closer. The closer router handles leads who have. The booking tag decides which one owns the lead, so the same call can never be treated as both.
3
Each outcome fires its own path
Warm outcomes start the follow up messages and nurture. Cold outcomes get one cross sell and then quiet. Confirmed after a closer call sends the deposit link. Unclear starts the 48 hour clock.
4
Guards stop double handling
A lead already in conversation, already booked, or already paid is recognised by every path and left alone.

🧩 The edges, and what happens there

🎙️
Voicemail or a few seconds of audio
A transcript with no real conversation in it cannot show a decision, so it lands in Unclear and a person looks at it. Missed calls never reach the classifier at all, they are routed by call status before it.
🗣️
Muddled speakers
The speaker labels are worked out from what each voice talks about, not from the phone line. If the transcript is too tangled to tell who committed to what, the honest answer is Unclear, not a guess.
🔁
A call that changes direction
The outcome is defined as the final decision or status of the call, so a prospect who wavers and then books is Confirmed, and one who warms up and then backs out is what they ended on.
🤷
When in doubt, it says so
Unclear is a designed outcome, not a failure. A wrong Confirmed or a wrong No would send a customer the wrong messages; an Unclear just costs a human two minutes.

🔍 Checking a decision

1
The transcript sits on the contact
Every decision can be read against the actual words, no listening back needed.
2
The Zap keeps a run history
Every classification, with what went in and what came out, is visible in the Zap's history for spot checks.
3
A wrong call is a two minute fix
Correct the tag on the contact and the system follows the correction. If the same mistake repeats, the written rules are tightened once and it stops.

🛡️ The safety net

1
Unclear is never lost
It still creates a task and a WhatsApp, and the 48 hour net alerts the team if nothing resolves.
2
The full transcript sits on the contact
Every decision can be checked against what was actually said.
3
Easy to change
The rules live in one place and take ten minutes to update.
