How Wave 2 thinks

Every Facebook lead travels the same road, and the system walks them down it. Understanding that one idea explains almost every rule in this manual.

The Wave 2 Facebook/Meta Ad pipeline in Opportunities is the road. Each card is a real person, each column a stage of their journey, and reading the board left to right is reading the state of the business today.

Why the cards move themselves
A stage change is never just a label. Landing in a stage fires that stage's messages, and leaving one switches them off. The automation moves a card only once it has recorded the real event, a call outcome, a booking, a payment, so what a customer hears always matches what actually happened to them. That is also why nobody drags cards by hand: a hand-moved card can fire the wrong messages at a real person, or silence the right ones, with no error shown anywhere.
Everyone · Chapter 2 of 5

Tags, the wiring

Tags look like labels. At BSUK they are how the automation remembers, and how it decides.

Every workflow decision, send this, skip that, wait, alert someone, is the system reading a contact's tags and branching on exactly what it finds. Two consequences follow. Tags are matched by exact spelling, one changed letter and a workflow silently stops finding anyone. And the system never removes tags on its own, so a contact's tag list is also their history, which later workflows rely on to know what has already happened.

System tags, green. Placed and read by the automation, hands off. 🟢

wave 2 facebook/meta lead wave 2 call booked amp call outcome is 'warm' closer call outcome is 'confirmed'

A few of the many you will see on Wave 2 contacts. Each one is a switch some workflow reads.

Tags people manage, honey outline. You may be asked to add one of these. 🖐️

💬 currently in conversation 🙋 human follow up needed

Exact spellings are being confirmed. Until then, add tags only when asked, typed exactly, all lowercase.

Why deleting is the dangerous one
A tag mistake is invisible. Nothing turns red, no error fires. A workflow quietly stops matching, a customer quietly stops hearing from us, and nobody finds out for weeks, usually when the customer calls to ask why we went silent. That is why the rule is blunt: never delete, never rename, add only when asked. It is not about trust, it is that the failure mode has no alarm.
Everyone · Chapter 3 of 5

The never-do list

Five things nobody does in GHL, whatever their role, even to fix something. Each one has an automation depending on it staying untouched.

🏷️
Never delete or rename a tag
Workflows match exact spellings and rely on old tags still being there. The failure is silent.
📈
Never drag a pipeline card
Stage changes fire and silence customer messages. The automation places every card for a reason.
⚙️
Never change anything in Automation
The workflows in there are the machinery under everything in this manual. Look freely, touch nothing.
📅
Never edit a calendar's settings
Booking flows, reminders and card moves all hang off the calendars being exactly as built.
📤
Never run a bulk action or mass send
One wrong filter and hundreds of customers hear from us at once. Bulk work goes through Nikki.
💬
And on WhatsApp, mind the window
Outside 24 hours of the customer's last message, only an approved template actually delivers. Typed messages silently fail.

Everyone · Chapter 5 of 5

When something looks odd

A card in a strange place, a message that should have sent and did not, a contact whose story does not add up. Whoever spots it, the drill is the same.

1
📷 Screenshot it as found
Before anything changes. The state of the screen at that moment is usually the whole diagnosis.
2
📝 Note the contact, the time, and how you got there
Those three details let Nikki replay exactly what the system did and find the wiring behind it.
3
💬 WhatsApp Nikki, then hands off
Not because you could not fix it, but because a hand fix hides the evidence. The odd thing is a symptom, and it needs to stay intact so the cause gets cured, not the case.
When in doubt, send it
A false alarm costs thirty seconds. A real problem caught early can save a customer. There is no version of this where sending was the wrong call.

The system does the remembering, the chasing and the routing. People do the noticing, the judgement and the conversations.

Office · Chapter 1 of 6

Log in as you

Everyone at BSUK works in GHL under their own login, never a shared inbox account. Everything else in the Office chapters depends on it.

Here is the why. Every click in GHL is stamped with the logged-in user. On a shared login, the system cannot tell one person's work from another's: it cannot give a task to you by name, cannot credit a call or a note to you, and cannot notify you about your own contacts, because as far as GHL knows, you do not exist.

📋
Tasks can find you
The system can put your name on a task, and only what is genuinely yours shows in your list.
🧭
Work is routed properly
Some things need Liam's authority, pricing, refunds, product calls. Separate logins keep his list holding only those, and yours holding the rest.
📊
The record tells the truth
Who called, who noted, who booked. When something needs unpicking later, the history says who actually did it.
🔒
And it is safer
Your own password, and shared logins stop being handed around.
From today
Always your login, never a shared account. If you find a browser signed in as a shared inbox, log out and back in as yourself before doing anything. The one minute it takes keeps the record honest.
Office · Chapter 2 of 6

Smart lists

A smart list is a search you save once and never run again, because it keeps itself current. Build one for any question you ask of the contact list more than once a week.

Any contact who matches the filter is in the list the moment they match, and out the moment they stop. The questions worth asking are the same every week: who has nobody looking after them, what came in this week, how are the ad leads doing. A smart list turns each into a tab at the top of Contacts, answered before you click it.

1
Open Contacts and click More Filters
The filter panel opens on the right, every field in the system is in there.
2
Pick the filter and its value
For example Assigned, left as empty. Watch the list narrow as you set it.
3
Save as smart list and name it
From then on it is a tab at the top of Contacts, permanently up to date.

Office · Chapter 3 of 6

Tasks, the moments the system asks for you

Wave 2 was built to need people as little as possible. Chases, reminders, follow-ups all run on their own. When the system creates a task, it has hit something only a human can do.

A lead replied after the chase gave up, a call needs making, a judgement needs a person. Tasks are rare by design, which is exactly why none should sit unseen: whatever is in the list is the only work the whole pipeline actually needs from a person that day. And because everyone is on their own login, your task list is truly yours.

Finding yours

1
Open Contacts in the left menu, then the Tasks tab along the top
Tasks live inside the Contacts area, in the row of tabs above the list, alongside Smart Lists.
2
Click the filter button at the top right of the task list
A panel opens with dropdowns for Assignee, Due date and Status.
3
Set Assignee to your name, Status to Pending, apply
The list is now only your open work, and the filter holds until you change it.

Working one

1
Open the task, then the contact it names

2
Do the thing, then mark the task completed
Completing matters as much as doing. An open task reads as undone work to both people and future automations.
3
If it is genuinely not yours, reroute it, never sit on it
Anything needing Liam's authority gets reassigned to him in the task's Assignee field. A task parked in the wrong list is invisible to the person who can actually do it.

Assigning contacts, one name on every person

Every contact has an Owner, the one person at BSUK responsible for them. Office keeps that field honest, because Office sees every conversation come through.

Why one name matters: the owner is who the system notifies when that contact does something, who calls and results are credited to, and who answers for them months later. An empty Owner field sends all three to nobody. The split works the other way too: Liam's name only on what genuinely needs him keeps his list short enough that he actually sees it.

For several at once, use the list

Office · Chapter 5 of 6

WhatsApp templates and the 24 hour rule

WhatsApp only lets a business message a customer freely within 24 hours of the customer's last message. Outside that window, only a pre-approved template will actually arrive.

The trap is how it fails. A typed message outside the window does not bounce or warn you. It shows as sent on our side and simply never reaches them, so the customer thinks we ignored them and we think we replied. Templates exist because Meta requires the wording of that outreach to be approved in advance, and once approved, a template always delivers, any time.

1
In the conversation, check the channel and the clock
WhatsApp selected as the channel, and a look at when the customer last wrote. Inside 24 hours, type freely. Outside it, template only.
2
Open the template picker in the message toolbar
Pick the one that fits, fill in any blanks it asks for, and send.
3
Check it shows delivered, not just sent
Delivered means it reached their phone. Sent alone, on an outside-window typed message, is the silent failure this chapter exists to avoid.
📷 Screenshot · Template picker
Screenshot slot: a WhatsApp conversation with the template picker open, and a delivered tick circled on a sent template
Where templates come from
Templates are written in GHL and submitted to Meta for approval, which can take minutes or a day or two, so we keep a set ready. The wording is customer-facing, so it follows the usual path: spot the gap, send Nikki the situation and rough wording, Liam signs off, Nikki builds and submits. Whoever runs the inbox is the best placed person in the business to spot the gap.

Sales · Chapter 1 of 4

The closer call

By the time a lead reaches your diary, the system has already warmed them, chased them and booked them. The call is yours, what you record afterwards is the system's.

Every closer call ends with an outcome, and the outcome is not paperwork. It is the switch that decides everything the customer hears next. Record it promptly after the call, because until you do, the system is waiting and the customer hears nothing.

✅
Confirmed
They are going ahead. The estimate path opens and the system starts working towards deposit.
🤔
Thinking
Genuinely deciding. A patient nurture sequence takes over, spaced so we stay present without crowding them.
🚫
No
Not for them. The system closes the loop politely and stops the sequences, so nobody gets chased after saying no.
❓
Unclear
Could not establish where they stand. The system flags these for follow-up rather than letting them drift.
Why promptness beats perfection
The outcome tags are read by workflows within minutes. A late outcome means a customer sitting in silence, and a forgotten one means a warm buyer treated like a stranger next week. Rough notes can be tidied later, the outcome goes in straight after the call.

Sales · Chapter 4 of 4

Your outcomes are tags

When you record a call outcome, what you are really doing is writing a tag the automation reads. Which is why the spelling discipline from the foundation applies doubly to you.

closer call outcome is 'confirmed' closer call outcome is 'thinking' closer call outcome is 'no' closer call outcome is 'unclear'

All lowercase, exact wording. These four switches decide which of four different futures the customer gets.

The honest outcome wins
An optimistic outcome does not help a sale, it mis-routes a person. Marking a thinking customer as confirmed sends them paperwork energy they are not ready for, and marking a no as thinking keeps messaging someone who asked to be done. The system amplifies whatever you record, so record what actually happened.

Call Team · Chapter 2 of 5

Call outcomes, and what each one triggers

Your outcome is a switch. The moment you record it, the system routes the lead down one of four roads, so the honest outcome is the helpful one.

🔥
Warm
Interested. The system starts nurture messages and works towards a sales team booking. Warm is for genuine interest, not politeness.
❌
No
Not for them. Sequences stop, a polite close goes out, and nobody chases someone who said no.
📵
No answer
The retry and chase machinery starts on its own. You do not need to remember them, the system does.
❓
Unclear
Could not establish where they stand. The system flags unclear outcomes for follow-up so they cannot quietly drift.
Why honest beats hopeful
Marking a polite-but-cold lead as warm does not help anyone. It books sales team time against someone who will not show, and it sends the lead messages that feel pushy because they never asked for them. The system amplifies whatever you record.
Call Team · Chapter 3 of 5

Booking the closer

A warm lead's next step is a diary slot with the sales team, and the booking itself is what moves their card forward. No booking, no movement.

1
Book while they are on the phone
A warm lead who hangs up without a slot has to be caught again later. The moment of interest is the moment to book.
2
Use the booking calendar, never a promise
A slot in the calendar triggers confirmations, reminders and the card move. A verbal we will call you Tuesday triggers nothing.
3
Confirm the details back
Day, time, and that it will be a call from the sales team at Barrel Sauna UK. Reminders go automatically, but hearing it said lands it.
Call Team · Chapter 4 of 5

Notes that help

Your note is what the next person opens before they speak to this lead. Write it for them.

💬
What they said, not a score
Wants a sauna for the garden office, asked about hot tubs too, beats seemed keen. Their words are the useful part.
📦
Which product came up
Sauna, hot tub, ice bath, glamping pod. It changes who should speak to them and what gets prepared.
🕰
When to catch them
Works shifts, only reachable after six. One line that saves three missed calls.
⚠️
Anything unusual
A complaint, a special request, a mention of a competitor. If it made you pause, it belongs in the note.
Call Team · Chapter 5 of 5

When to hand a caller to BSUK

Some things are not for the first call to solve, and passing them on quickly is the professional move, not a failure.

1
Pricing beyond the published list, discounts, refunds
These need BSUK authority. Take the details, promise a call back from the team, record the outcome honestly.
2
Upset or complaining callers
Acknowledge, take details, do not defend or explain the company's side. A named person from BSUK will come back to them.
3
Never guess an answer
Build details, delivery timelines, technical specifics. A wrong answer travels further than a we will find out for you.
One name to remember
To customers, the founder is James. If a caller asks for the owner or mentions James, that is normal, route it through the handover as usual.

Every rule in this manual exists for the same reason. So the customer's experience is true to what actually happened to them.
