One change before anything else: from today, you work in GHL under your own login, not info@barrel. Everything else in this guide depends on it.

Here is the why. Every click in GHL is stamped with the logged-in user. While you work as info@barrel, the system cannot tell your work from Liam's: it cannot give a task to you by name, cannot credit a call or a note to you, and cannot notify you about your own contacts, because as far as GHL knows, you do not exist. You have been doing the work while the record says Liam did it.

📋 Tasks can find you
The system can put your name on a task, and only what is genuinely yours shows in your list. Section 5 is built on this.
🧭 Work is routed properly
Some things need Liam's authority, pricing, refunds, product calls. With separate logins, his list holds only those, and yours holds the rest. That split is the whole point.
📊 The record tells the truth
Who called, who noted, who booked. When something needs unpicking later, the history says who actually did it.
🔒 And it is safer
Your own password, and the shared inbox login stops being handed around. Everyone at BSUK is moving to this, you are first.

A smart list is a search you save once and never run again, because it keeps itself current. Any contact who matches the filter is in it the moment they match, and out the moment they stop.

Why that matters: the questions worth asking of the contact list are the same ones every week. Who has nobody looking after them? What came in this week? How are the ad leads doing? Searching for those over and over is wasted minutes and easy to forget. A smart list turns each question into a tab at the top of Contacts, answered before you click it.

1
👤 Open Contacts and click More Filters
The filter panel opens on the right, every field in the system is in there.
2
🎯 Pick the filter and its value
For example Assigned, left as empty. Watch the list narrow as you set it.
3
💾 Save as smart list and name it
From then on it is a tab at the top of Contacts, permanently up to date.

WHY WE NEVER DRAG A CARD
Two reasons, both invisible at the moment of dragging. First, dropping a card into a stage can fire that stage's messages at a customer whose story does not match them, or silence a sequence they should be getting. Second, the automation writes its own record of events, so a hand-moved card is often shoved straight back minutes later, and now the history is muddled too. A card in the wrong place is telling you something real about the system. Screenshot it and send it to Nikki, that is genuinely more useful than fixing it. 📷

THE SHAPE OF WAVE 2
The pipeline runs itself. A task is the rare moment it asks for a person. ✅

The Wave 2 pipeline was built to need people as little as possible. Chases, reminders, follow-ups, all of it runs on its own. So when the system creates a task, it means it has hit something only a human can do: a lead replied after the chase gave up, a call needs making, a judgement needs a person. Tasks are rare by design, which is exactly why none of them should sit unseen.

And this is why section 2 came first. Now that you have your own login, the system can put a task against your name, and your task list becomes a true list of what is genuinely yours, not a pile shared with Liam's.

🔧 WORKING A TASK
1
Open the task, then the contact it names
Every task is pinned to a contact, so the full story, messages, notes, history, is one click away. Read it before acting, the task only makes sense in its context.
2
Do the thing, then mark the task completed
Completing it matters as much as doing it. An open task reads as undone work, and if we later automate reminders off overdue tasks, a finished-but-open task would nag someone for nothing.
3
If a task is genuinely not yours, reroute it, never sit on it
Anything needing Liam's authority, pricing, refunds, product calls, gets reassigned to him using the task's Assignee field, or a quick WhatsApp to Nikki if you are unsure. A task parked in the wrong list is invisible to the person who can actually do it.

Every contact has an Owner, the one person at BSUK responsible for them. Now that everyone is under their own login, ownership finally means what it says, and you are the natural person to keep it right, because you see every conversation come through.

Why one name matters: the owner is who the system notifies when that contact does something, who the calls and results are credited to, and who answers for them when a question comes up months later. An empty Owner field means all three of those go to nobody. And the split works the other way too: putting Liam's name only on what genuinely needs him, pricing, refunds, product decisions, keeps his list short enough that he actually sees it.

You see tags on every contact. What is easy to miss is what they are for: tags are how the automation remembers. Every workflow decision, send this, skip that, wait, alert someone, is the system reading a contact's tags and branching on exactly what it finds.

Two consequences follow. First, tags are matched by exact spelling, one changed letter and a workflow simply stops finding anyone, with no error shown anywhere. Second, the system never removes tags on its own, so a contact's tag list is also their history, and the automation relies on old tags still being there to know what has already happened to someone.

WHY DELETING IS THE DANGEROUS ONE
A tag mistake is invisible. Nothing turns red, no error fires. A workflow quietly stops matching, a customer quietly stops hearing from us, and nobody finds out for weeks, usually when the customer calls to ask why we went silent. That is why the rule is blunt: never delete, never rename, add only when asked. It is not about trust, it is that the failure mode has no alarm. 👀

You run the inbox, so this rule touches you more than anyone: WhatsApp only lets a business message a customer freely within 24 hours of the customer's last message. Outside that window, only a pre-approved template will actually arrive.

The trap is how it fails. A typed message outside the window does not bounce or warn you. It shows as sent on our side and simply never reaches them, so the customer thinks we ignored them and we think we replied. Templates exist because Meta requires the wording of that first outreach message to be approved in advance, and once approved, a template always delivers, any time.

📤 USING A TEMPLATE
1
In the conversation, check the channel and the clock
WhatsApp selected as the channel, and a look at when the customer last wrote. Inside 24 hours, type freely. Outside it, template only.
2
Open the template picker in the message toolbar
Pick the one that fits, fill in any blanks it asks for, and send.
3
Check it shows delivered, not just sent
Delivered means it reached their phone. Sent alone, on an outside-window typed message, is the silent failure this whole section exists to avoid.

🔧 WHERE TEMPLATES COME FROM

Templates are written in GHL and submitted to Meta for approval, which can take anything from minutes to a day or two, so we keep a set ready rather than writing them when we need them. The wording is customer-facing, so it follows the usual path: when you notice a message you keep needing and no template fits, send Nikki the situation and rough wording, Liam signs off the copy, Nikki builds and submits it. Your part is spotting the gap, and you are the best placed person in the business to spot it.

ONE LAST THING
You know this system's normal rhythm better than anyone. That is exactly why you will notice when it is off. 👀

A card in a strange place, a message that should have gone and did not, a contact whose story does not add up. You will spot these before anyone else, and what you do next is the difference between a five minute fix and a customer lost.

1
📷 Screenshot it as found
Before anything changes. The state of the screen at that moment is usually the whole diagnosis.
2
📝 Note the contact, the time, and how you got there
Those three details let Nikki replay exactly what the system did and find the wiring behind it.
3
💬 WhatsApp Nikki, then hands off
Not because you could not fix it, but because a hand fix hides the evidence. The odd thing you found is a symptom, and Nikki needs it intact to cure the cause rather than the case.
WHEN IN DOUBT, SEND IT
A false alarm costs thirty seconds. A real problem caught early can save a customer. There is no version of this where sending was the wrong call. 👍
